> "Bring code to the data instead of data to the code." — Onur Mutlu, on the philosophy driving memory-centric computing

I am a research intern in the **CSArch Lab at Korea University**, working toward
admission to a Master's program in Spring 2027. My research interests lie at the
intersection of **computer architecture, memory systems, memory-centric computing,
and energy-efficient system design**. I am fundamentally motivated by a simple
observation shared by many in our community: modern computing is no longer bound
by computation, but by the cost of moving data. My goal is to help close that gap
by designing architectures and system-level techniques that bring computation
closer to where data lives, rather than the other way around.

My research experience so far has centered on hardware-aware performance and
efficiency modeling. As a research intern at the **PSAL Lab, POSTECH** (advised by
Prof. Gwangsun Kim), I extended PyTorchSim by implementing
support for a new ATen operator through custom lowerings and MLIR-based execution
paths, and authored a contributor-facing tutorial to lower the barrier for future
MLIR-backed operator development. As an undergraduate researcher in the **AIAC Lab
at Hallym University** (advised by Prof. Jeong-Gun Lee), I studied Edge AI inference
performance, building regression-based models to predict delay, power, energy, and
EDP, and constructed an experimental measurement pipeline on Jetson Orin Nano to
capture fine-grained runtime and power data. This work led to a first-author
publication at **IEEE ICCE 2026**, *"Predicting Delay, Power, and Energy of Edge AI
Inference with Ridge Regression."*

Looking ahead, I want to move from measuring the cost of data movement to actively
redesigning systems that minimize it — through domain-specific accelerators,
near-memory and in-memory computation, and heterogeneous computing architectures
that treat energy efficiency as a first-class design constraint. I believe the next
generation of computer systems will be defined not by raw compute throughput, but
by how intelligently they manage the flow of data, and I hope to contribute to that
shift through graduate research.

Outside the lab, I have participated in national ICT competitions and startup
programs, which have sharpened my ability to translate research ideas into working
prototypes under real constraints. I am always glad to connect with fellow
researchers, especially those interested in memory systems, energy-efficient
computing, or graduate school life — feel free to reach out.
